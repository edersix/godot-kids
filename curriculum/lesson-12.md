# Lesson 12: My Own Game (Part 2) and Showcase

> Duration: about 45 minutes | Ages 8–10 | Godot 4.x

## Goal
"Today I will finish my game and show it to my friends."

## Big Idea (Concept)
Making a game is a loop: **plan, build, test, share**. Players give feedback and the game gets better.

## New Words
| Word | Kid-friendly meaning |
|------|----------------------|
| Playtest | Let someone else play your game to see what they think |
| Feedback | What a player says about your game |
| Showcase | A time to show your work to others |
| Developer | A person who makes games or programs |

## Materials
- The project each child worked on in lesson 11 (do **not** use the lesson 12 starter unless a child's project no longer runs)
- Starter project: `projects/lesson-12/starter` (the same as the lesson 11 starter, for a child who needs a fresh start)
- Solution project: `projects/lesson-12/solution` (an example finished remix)
- Printed certificates and skills checklists from [`../assessment/`](../assessment/)
- Parent handout: `parent-handouts/lesson-12.md`
- Slides: `slides/lesson-12.md`

## Timing
| Minutes | Activity |
|---------|----------|
| 0–3 | Recap and goal for today |
| 3–23 | Build time: finish and polish your game |
| 23–35 | Playtest: swap seats and play a friend's game, then give feedback |
| 35–40 | Showcase: each child shows or tells about their game |
| 40–45 | Certificates, celebration and wrap-up |

## Steps
1. Reopen your lesson 11 project. Read your Game Design Card. **Checkpoint:** every child knows what they want to finish today.
2. Build and test for about 20 minutes. Remind kids to save often. **Checkpoint:** the game runs from start to Game Over without errors.
3. Playtest: kids move to a neighbor's laptop and play for 3 minutes.
4. Feedback rules: each player says **one thing they liked** and **one idea**. Nobody says "that is bad". **Checkpoint:** every child has heard one good thing about their game.
5. Showcase: each child says their game's name and one sentence about it (use the projector for a few volunteers).
6. Hand out certificates. Take a group photo only if the organization's child-photo policy and the parents' permissions allow it.

## Make It Yours
This lesson is entirely "make it yours". Suggested extras:
- Add a second kind of coin or enemy.
- Change the Game Over message to fit the story.
- Add a title to the game with a new Label.

## If You Get Stuck
1. "Open your Game Design Card. Which part is not working yet?"
2. "Undo with Ctrl+Z to go back to a version that worked."
3. If the project is broken, use the lesson 12 starter and redo the best change. It is better to show a small working game than a big broken one.
4. Look at the solution project.

## Facilitator Notes
- The showcase is the most important part. Make sure all children get a turn, even if short.
- Optional extension for curious kids: if someone asks what a "class" is, answer "a scene is a blueprint, and every copy in the game is made from it". This is the idea behind object-oriented programming. Do not teach the vocabulary.
- A child whose game is broken can show a screenshot of a version that worked, or demonstrate the lesson 12 starter with their changes. Never let anyone feel they failed.
- Complete the skills checklist for each child while they work (see [`../assessment/`](../assessment/)). Do not let kids see a comparison with others.
- Remind kids what they can do at home (see the parent handout).
- Collect feedback from kids (what they liked most) and from parents after the course.

## Wrap-Up
- Recap question: "What are you most proud of?"
- Celebrate with applause for each child.
- Hand out the final parent handout.
